# VAULTIS Round 2 Security & Blockchain Gap Analysis
Generated: 2026-09-08 | Branches reviewed: `main` (commit `318029a`), `devansh-backend` (diff vs main inspected; stale, deletes 4021 lines, focus remains on `main` as production behavior)

## Executive Summary
VAULTIS's core differentiator — PostgreSQL-fresh, pre-retrieval `$in` filtering before ChromaDB (`backend/app/rag.py:26-34` → `backend/app/rag.py:154`) — is structurally sound and survives prompt-injection analysis. Overall posture is **demo-ready but not production-hardened**: cryptography is correctly chosen (AES-256-GCM, Argon2id) and the SHA-256 chain is tamper-evident, yet session management, transport hardening, input validation, and monitoring remain at hackathon-prototype level. The single biggest risk is **no session revocation or login rate limiting** (`backend/app/auth.py:14-44`, `backend/app/main.py:50-85`) — a stolen JWT is valid for up to 8 hours. The single biggest pivot opportunity is **honest blockchain framing**: the ledger today is centralized and self-attested; a one-day Merkle-root anchor to a public testnet would let you claim verifiable anchoring without rebuilding the chain.

## Blockchain Pivot Assessment

### Would the current implementation survive "why do you call this blockchain, where is the chain/consensus/decentralization?"

**No — not as a blockchain claim.** What exists today is a **centralized, tamper-evident SHA-256 hash chain** stored in a single PostgreSQL table (`backend/app/audit.py:8-52`, `backend/app/models.py:51-59`, `backend/alembic/versions/0001_initial_schema.py:28`). It has sequencing and hash linking (git/Certificate Transparency semantics) and a recomputation verifier (`backend/app/audit.py:44-52`), but it has none of the DLT properties a judge in the *Blockchain & Cybersecurity* track will probe for:

- **No decentralization:** one writer (your FastAPI process), one store (your Postgres). Anyone with superuser/DB backup access can rewrite history.
- **No independent verifiability:** verification requires trusting the same DB that stores the chain. A judge or auditor cannot verify without your server.
- **No consensus / append-only guarantee:** Postgres can `UPDATE` or `DELETE` a row and re-hash successors; the `UniqueConstraint(record_hash)` (`backend/app/models.py:59`) helps only against accidental collisions, not a full rewrite (`UPDATE audit_chain SET payload=..., record_hash=...` for every row from the tampered point forward).
- **No trusted timestamp:** `timestamp: String(100)` (`backend/app/models.py:57`) is self-asserted wall-clock time (`datetime.now(timezone.utc).isoformat()` in `backend/app/audit.py:31`); no RFC 3161 timestamp authority.

The strongest honest framing today is:

> **"Cryptographically tamper-evident, blockchain-inspired audit ledger — centralized hash chain with full recomputation verification. Not yet a distributed ledger."**

Claiming `blockchain` without anchoring will be challenged and will undermine the otherwise strong structural-filtering story.

### What a real DLT-backed evidence system would need

- Append-only ledger with **external commitment** (hash anchored outside your trust domain).
- Multi-party verifiability (judge/auditor can verify without your server).
- Optionally, **smart-contract-enforced access rules** as a complement to (not replacement for) the PostgreSQL filter — e.g., on-chain disclosure approvals.

### 3 buildable-in-hackathon-timeframe options to close the gap

| Option | What it adds | Effort | What you can claim on stage that you cannot claim today |
|--------|--------------|--------|----------------------------------------------------------|

**Recommendation for Round 2:** ship **Option A** (Merkle anchor) and keep **Option C** as the fallback if testnet funds/RPC stall. Show the anchor tx live, then walk the verifier. Retire the word "blockchain" from the landing page copy (`frontend/src/components/LandingPage.tsx:52` today says "tamper-evident ledger" — keep that) and introduce "blockchain-anchored" only after you can point to a tx hash. If you cannot ship an anchor before judging, use the honest framing above and place Option A on the roadmap slide — it is more credible than an unbacked blockchain label.

### What would be needed to earn the stronger "blockchain" claim (vs "blockchain-inspired")

- At least one external, **independent trust root** (public chain, consortium peers, or RFC 3161 TSA) that an auditor can check without your infrastructure.
- Published smart-contract address + verifier that proves `record_hash` inclusion.
- Documented threat model: what an attacker with full DB access **cannot** do after anchoring (re-hash attack now requires rewriting the external chain).
- Until then, label the system "SHA-256 hash chain, blockchain-anchored" only after anchoring is live; before that, "blockchain-inspired" is the defensible term.
